Before you rely on this

This is an estimate. It uses the April 2026 rates and the main rules, but a real award turns on your exact circumstances, your assessment period dates, and things a web page cannot see. Check it against entitledto or Turn2us, both free, before making any decision.
Savings over £16,000 stop a claim. Between £6,000 and £16,000 each £250 above the first £6,000, or part of £250, is treated as producing £4.35 a month of income whether it does or not.
Deductions are not shown. Repayments of an advance, rent or council tax arrears, and overpayments all come off the payment after this calculation, and can take several hundred pounds a month.
The housing figure is a ceiling. It is the most that can be paid towards rent. If your rent is higher, the difference is yours to find — and the shortfall is the single most common reason renters on Universal Credit fall into arrears.
Getting it wrong costs money. If a claim is refused or paid at less than you expected, that is worth challenging — ask for a mandatory reconsideration within a month. Citizens Advice will help you do it, free.
