Full explanation

If your landlord or a court applies statutory interest to rent arrears, you should only pay interest on what is actually left to pay — not the original lump sum. Standard debt calculators charge interest on the whole lump sum all the way through, which overcharges you.

This calculator charges flat, non-compounding simple interest (like the UK statutory 8% rate) on the shrinking balance, payment by payment — monthly, or weekly, fortnightly or quarterly if that is how you pay. Every payment brings the balance down, and the interest with it. Interest is never charged on interest.

It runs until the debt reaches nil and shows the number of payments, the final odd-sized payment, and the true interest cost. If the landlord is charging no interest, leave the rate at 0 and it simply shows how long your plan takes.

If a landlord turns down a mathematically correct repayment plan, showing that you tried to set a reasonable schedule can help you — but only if the numbers are completely accurate. Check them with Citizens Advice or Shelter before you rely on them.

How it is worked out. A decreasing principal sum. At each payment date, simple interest at 0% a year is added for the days since the last date (balance × rate × days ÷ 365). The payment clears that interest first and the rest comes off the balance, so the next period's interest is charged on a smaller sum. Interest is never charged on interest. The last payment is only what is left.
