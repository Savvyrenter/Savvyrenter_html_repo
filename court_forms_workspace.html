How a small money claim works for renters (England and Wales)

What it is for

Claiming money a landlord or letting agent owes you: a deposit or holding deposit that was not returned, rent you overpaid, the cost of belongings damaged by disrepair, or fees that were banned. A claim for compensation because a deposit was never protected is made a different way, through the county court rather than the online money claim service, so get advice first for that one.

Before you claim

Write to the landlord or agent first: set out what is owed, why, how you worked it out, and a reasonable date to pay by (14 days is common). Keep proof you sent it. A court expects you to have tried this, and can hold it against you if you skipped it.

Making the claim

The claim is made online through the government’s money claim service. There is a court fee, which depends on how much you claim; you get it back from the other side if you win. You can also claim interest at 8% a year under section 69 of the County Courts Act 1984 — the interest calculator works it out.

After you claim

The landlord or agent has 14 days to respond, or 28 if they first acknowledge the claim. If they do nothing, you can ask the court for judgment without a hearing. If they defend it, claims of up to £10,000 normally go on the small claims track: you fill in a Directions Questionnaire, most cases are first referred to the free Small Claims Mediation Service by phone, and if that does not settle it there is a hearing. Hearings are informal, and each side usually pays its own costs.

Evidence to gather

The tenancy agreement; the deposit certificate and the deposit information you were given; the check-in and check-out inventories and your own dated photos; bank statements showing what you paid (redact them first); every letter, email and text, in date order; receipts and quotes for any costs; and a short timeline of what happened when.

If you win and they still don’t pay

The court can enforce the judgment, for example through bailiffs or by taking money from the other side’s earnings or bank account. Each method has its own fee, which is added to what they owe.
