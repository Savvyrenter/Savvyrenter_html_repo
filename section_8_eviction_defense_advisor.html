Section 8 Notice Checker

England · for notices given on or after 1 May 2026 (Renters' Rights Act 2025) · checks the notice, the grounds and the defences to raise
A Section 8 notice is not an eviction. It is the landlord saying they may apply to court. You do not have to leave when it runs out. Only a court order followed by court bailiffs can make you leave — anyone who forces you out, changes the locks or cuts off your supply without that is committing a crime (Protection from Eviction Act 1977). What to do next

  1. Stay put and keep paying what you can. Keep proof of every payment. Paying down arrears before the hearing can defeat Ground 8.
  2. Get free advice now — Shelter, Citizens Advice or a Law Centre. If you are on a low income you may get free legal aid for possession cases.
  3. If court papers arrive, reply within 14 days using the defence form that comes with them (Form N11R). Put every point from this page in it.
  4. Go to the hearing. Free on-the-day legal help is available at court through the Housing Loss Prevention Advice Service — ask the court staff.
  5. Tell your council if you might lose your home within 8 weeks: they have a legal duty to help prevent homelessness.
