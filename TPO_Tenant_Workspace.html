TPO Complaint Workspace

Draft it here at your own pace · paste it into the portal when it is ready
Untested, unverified and possibly incomplete. This workspace has not been checked end to end against the live service. Treat everything it produces as a draft, and check it against the real form before you rely on it. Found a step we have not covered? Save that page from The Property Ombudsman portal before you type anything into it (Ctrl+S, or Cmd+S on a Mac, “Webpage, complete”), run it through our web page data scrubber, and email it to us so we can build it in. We aim to do that within a couple of days, but this is a volunteer project and that is an aim, not a promise.
The Property Ombudsman is the other redress scheme letting agents can belong to. Its portal hides the case forms entirely until a signed Letter of Authority is attached, and it will not save a part-finished complaint — so a long submission has to be written in one sitting or lost. This page is the same sequence, with the same character limits, kept on your own computer. Write it over days if you need to, save as you go, then paste the finished text into the real portal in one go. Nothing you type here is stored or sent anywhere. It is not connected to The Property Ombudsman and submits nothing — the real complaint is still made on their site.

0 · Is the agent a TPO member?

Worth two minutes before you write anything else. This scheme can only look at a complaint about one of its own members — if the agent is not one, everything you write here would go nowhere.

Opens in a new tab. Dismiss any cookie banner, then search for the agent by the name on your tenancy agreement or letterhead, including the Ltd — trading names often differ from the registered one.

If they are not listed. Every letting agent in England must belong to a redress scheme, and there are two. Try the Property Redress Scheme next — if that is where they are, your complaint goes to them instead and this page is the wrong form. If they are in neither, they are trading unlawfully, and your council's private-sector housing team can act on that.

1 · Eligibility

The Ombudsman will normally only look at a complaint once the agent's own procedure has been exhausted, and there are time limits after their final answer — so these two dates matter.

2 · Letter of authority

This is the gate. The real portal hides every case form until a signed Letter of Authority is attached. Nothing past this step opens until you confirm you have one — the same way it will happen on the day.
