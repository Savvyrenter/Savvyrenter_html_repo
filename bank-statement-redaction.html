Bank statement redaction

Black out what nobody else needs to see, before you hand a statement over
A statement proves what you paid. It also carries your account number, your sort code, and every other payment you have ever made — none of which a landlord, an agent or an opponent is entitled to see. This blacks them out properly: the marks are burned into the picture, so there is nothing underneath to copy out again. Your file never leaves this computer. What to leave on show

A rough guide for a rent dispute. The point of handing over a statement is to prove you paid. Cover too much and it proves nothing; cover too little and you have handed over your whole life. This is what has worked, not a rule of law.

It depends on what is in dispute

There is no fixed list, and this is the part that matters most. Work out what the other side is likely to say, then leave showing whatever answers it — even where you would rather cover it.

  • You say you paid. Leave every payment out to them, in full, with the date and the amount.
  • They say they paid you back. Leave every credit into the account for that period, with the name of whoever sent it showing. Cover the sources and they can point at any deposit and say “that was us”. With the sources on show it is plain on the face of the statement that none of them was.
  • They say you underpaid in a particular month. Leave that whole month, both columns, not only the one line you are relying on. A single line lifted out of a covered page invites the question of what is around it.
  • They rely on a document of their own. Leave whatever of yours meets it head on, in the same period.

So tailor it to your own case. The same figure can help you on one issue and hurt you on another, and only you know what is being argued about — the decision is yours, and this page is a guide, not a ruling.

Leave it showing

  • Every rent payment — the date, the amount, and who it went to. That is the evidence. Never cover it.
  • The running balance column. A balance that runs unbroken down the page shows nothing has been taken out of the middle.
  • Your name and address at the top. Your landlord or agent already has both, so there is nothing to protect, and they tie the account to you.
  • The last four digits of the account number, the bank’s name, the statement period and the page numbers (“page 4 of 37”). The last four are how the account is identified in evidence without giving the number away.

Cover it

  • The rest of the account number, and the sort code.
  • Everything you spent that has nothing to do with the dispute — the payee and the amount. An intact column of figures can be read out in a hearing and turned into a story about you.
  • Other people’s names, references and account details, including anyone who pays money in.
  • Card numbers, telephone numbers, and anything that identifies a third party.

Never do this

  • Do not crop or delete rows. Cover them. A statement with lines removed looks edited, and the balance will not add up.
  • Do not use a highlighter, a shape in Word, or a black box you can drag off. Only a mark burned into the picture is a redaction. That is what this page does.
  • Do not hand over your only copy. Keep the original, unredacted, somewhere safe — the court can order it.

Before you send it

Open the saved file and read it as if you were the other side. Then ask the court, or the other side’s solicitor, whether what you have sent is acceptable — it is far better to be told now than at a hearing. If you cover something the other side says they need, they can ask the court to order an unredacted copy, and the court decides.

We are not lawyers and this is not legal advice. It is one renter’s experience of preparing documents for a county court claim, written down in case it helps. Rules differ between courts and between kinds of case. If anything turns on it, get advice — Shelter, Citizens Advice and your local Law Centre are free.
